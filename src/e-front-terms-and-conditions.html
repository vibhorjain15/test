Supplementary measures in addition to the Standard Contractual Clauses applicable to the Processing of Personal Information by BlackRock

  1. Challenge to legally binding requests

Without prejudice to any existing confidentiality obligations owed by BlackRock to the Client (or vice versa), in the event BlackRock receives a legally binding order, request or similar from any third party for compelled disclosure of any Personal Information that has been transferred under the Standard Contractual Clauses (a “Request”), BlackRock shall promptly communicate such Request to the Client to the extent it concerns BPI, unless BlackRock is prohibited by law from providing such notification. To the extent BlackRock is prohibited by law from providing such notification, BlackRock shall: (i) review each Request on a case-by-case basis; (ii) use best efforts (which shall not necessarily include any formal legal proceedings) to obtain that the confidentiality requirement be waived to enable BlackRock to notify the Client of the Request; and (iii) maintain evidence of any such attempt to have a confidentiality requirement waived. In any event, where receiving a Request, BlackRock shall:

  1. use all reasonable endeavours to redirect the third party to request data directly from the Client;
  2. to the extent that, if after careful consideration, BlackRock reasonably concludes that there are valid grounds exist under the law applicable to the requesting third party to do so, challenge the Request on the basis of any legal deficiencies under the laws of the requesting party or any relevant conflicts with the laws applicable in the jurisdiction(s) from which the transfer was first made; and
  3. make no disclosure of such Personal Information until mandatorily or legally required to do so under applicable procedural rules or legal requirements,

and where BlackRock is not prohibited from providing a notification of a Request to the Client, the Client shall bear BlackRock’s reasonable costs of complying with its obligations under paragraphs 4.6.1 to 4.6.3.

  1. Prohibition on handover of encryption keys in respect of Personal Information transferred to the United States of America (if applicable)

BlackRock shall at all times refrain from handing over, communicating or making available to any government body, law enforcement agency or similar, in each case where established in the United States of America, the encryption keys used by BlackRock or by the Client to decrypt Personal Information in transit by BlackRock on behalf of the Client. To the extent legally permitted, BlackRock shall immediately inform the Client in writing where BlackRock intends to decrypt Personal Information Processed on behalf of the Client in order to comply with a disclosure request issued by such a third party.

  1. Compliance with state of the art encryption standards

Subject to the technical capabilities afforded by the Client’s browser, and where Personal Information is in transit from the EEA, the UK or Switzerland to locations outside of the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, BlackRock shall ensure that the encryption measures it implements to protect the Personal Information of the Client so transferred use encryption of at least AES-128, AES-192 or AES-256 standard, or otherwise encryption which otherwise complies with paragraph 3.2.4 of the ENISA 2020 “Guideline “State of the Art”: Technical and Organisational Measures”.
